design spikes · round 3

Remix Deck

Four working spikes exploring the remix deck's next form. The brief: the original intent was a place you can spin multiple decks; the right-side FX rack is good and stays; the voice pads never worked and need a rethink. Each spike is a standalone page that runs on your real crate (or synthesises demo records if the crate is empty) and composes one balanced Strudel program per state change.

Why the current voices fail

Diagnosed from src/app.js: each vox pad re-evaluates the entire program to add a bare s("yeah:1") layer — which loops, unquantised, from wherever the scheduler happens to be — then a setTimeout re-evaluates again a cycle later to remove it. Two audible hiccups per stab, random timing, and spoken samples with no relationship to the record's key or tempo. Every spike below bans that pattern: everything latches, loops on the grid, and re-evaluates only on deliberate toggles.

The spikes

01 · Twin Spinremix-twin-spin.html

The intent in its purest form: a classic two-deck rig for saved records. No voice at all.

  • Record shelf with per-sleeve →A / →B load buttons; two platters wearing their vinyl discs.
  • Equal-power crossfader baked into each bed's .gain(); per-deck transition filter.
  • One master tempo (deck A's cps + ±8% nudge) — records are cycle-based, so retempoing is natural.
  • The proven FX rack (sweep / stutter / riser) applied at master level.
loads cleanbalanced programsno voice — deliberately
The cleanest answer to “spin multiple decks”. Everything here survives into Live Wire.

02 · Voice Boothremix-voice-booth.html

Single bed, maximum voice rework — three reliable voice engines side by side.

  • Phrase pads: latching one-bar patterned vocal lines (s("~ yeah:1 ~ [yeah:1 ho:3]")) — always on the grid.
  • Chop line: a longer spoken sample sliced rhythmically (.slow(2).chop(8) + delay), two flavours.
  • Key-locked choir: sample-free “aah/ooh” — n().scale(recordKey).s("sawtooth").vowel("<a o e o>") — always in tune with the record, zero sample risk.
  • Audition mode: tap a pad while stopped to read its pattern before committing.
loads cleanbalanced programsdensest UI of the four
The choir is the standout idea — it cannot be out of key and needs no samples. The phrase pads prove quantised latching fixes the old timing mess.

03 · Spin Wallremix-spin-wall.html

The maximal take: four records at once. How far does layering go before it turns to mud?

  • 2×2 wall of mini-decks, each with gain, mute, and a three-way FOCUS switch (FULL / LOWS .lpf(400) / TOPS .hpf(1200)) — EQ-kill mixing so one record supplies the floor and another the tops.
  • Master tempo clock + the FX rack across every bed.
loads cleanbalanced at 4 beds (~2.9 KB programs)musical ceiling unknown until auditioned
A genuine experiment, not a ship candidate. Two arranged records already collide in long-form structure; four needs the FOCUS kills doing constant work. Worth ten minutes of ear time to learn the ceiling, then likely folds back to two decks.

04 · Live Wireremix-live-wire.html

The ship candidate: Twin Spin's rig + a real mixer channel + only the voice ideas that earn their place.

  • Two decks, equal-power crossfader, and per-channel LO KILL / HI KILL + gain trim — the classic DJ mixer.
  • Master tempo nudge + the proven FX rack.
  • Exactly two voice features: a hook line pad (three latching quantised flavours, re-tap to cycle) and the choir pad (key-locked, sample-free).
  • Press banks the current remix program back into the crate as a new record.
loads cleanbalanced programspress-to-crate round trip
The balanced integration — multiple decks, the good FX, and voice reduced to the two ideas that are reliable by construction.

Recommendation: integrate Live Wire as the new remix deck — it delivers the multi-deck intent, keeps the FX rack untouched, and replaces the broken vox grid with the two voice features that cannot mistime (latching quantised hook line, key-locked choir). If deeper voice play still appeals after that lands, Voice Booth's phrase-pad panel drops in as an expansion. Spin Wall stays a spike; audition it once for the answer to “how many decks is too many”.

Caveats before committing: all four are verified headlessly (no page errors, balanced programs under control-mashing) but unauditioned — ears required, especially .vowel() timbre and two arranged 36-bar records phasing against each other. Loop-format presses mix tighter than arranged ones; a future “re-loop this record” toggle on load would help.
