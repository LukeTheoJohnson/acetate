The Authentic Deck

Round 3 landed on Live Wire — two crate records on a real mixer (kills, trims, equal-power crossfader, FX rack). You liked it, and Twin Spin alongside. The brief for round 4: make it feel like what a real amateur DJ actually wants — authentic and realistic, not a toy or an "auto" button. This round reviews where the two-deck rig still falls short of real DJing, then spikes four ways to close the gap. Each spike keeps the Live Wire chassis and pushes one axis of authenticity.

What Live Wire / Twin Spin already get right

The iconography is correct: two decks + an equal-power crossfader is the single most recognisable "this is DJing" signal, and the maths is right (cos/sin gains). EQ kills and the filter fade map onto the two highest-reward, lowest-skill beginner moves — the bass swap and the filter sweep — which is exactly what makes a beginner feel like a DJ fastest. One master tempo means tracks never clash. That's a strong base. The gaps below are about the craft on top of it.

The gaps vs. a real amateur DJ

1 · No sense of when to mix. Real DJ software's defining visual isn't the platter — it's the scrolling waveform + beatgrid + phrase markers, because that's how you time a transition to a 16/32-bar boundary. Our disc is decorative; it carries no timing. You can't phrase-match by eye. biggest gap
2 · Sync is 100% invisible → zero agency. Everything retempos to one cps, so there is no beatmatch moment. That is the exact "auto button feels hollow" trap. The craft isn't the maths — it's choosing the moment and the move.
3 · The bass swap is a binary kill. A hard LOW KILL toggle is cruder than the real move: a smooth, simultaneous low-EQ cross. The #1 beginner move deserves a gesture, not a switch.
4 · No stems — despite owning them. djay's flagship beginner feature is Neural Mix: split a track into drums / bass / vocals / harmonics and mix the parts. This app composes those parts — so it can offer exact, offline, free stem control that pros pay AI to fake.
5 · No cue, no performance moves. No auditioning deck B before the fade; no loop roll, reload, or spinback. FX are set-and-forget latching toggles, not beat-synced hold-to-fire paddles.
6 · Voice was over-weighted. Rounds 1–3 chased the vox pads, but acapella layering is an advanced move. For an amateur, crossfade + bass swap + filter + phrase-timing are the wins.

The four spikes

01 · Phrase Deckremix-phrase-deck.html

The rig gains a real DJ's sense of TIME. The phrase timeline becomes the primary visual; the disc shrinks to an identity badge.

  • A scrolling 32-bar phrase/beat timeline with a playhead, 8/16/32 boundaries, and a MIX NOW light that pulses in the last bar before a boundary.
  • ARM →A / →B quantises the transition: the crossfade fires exactly on the next 8/16/32-bar line — slam, or auto-fade over the bars leading in.
  • Closes gaps 1 & 2 — you finally mix on the phrase, and the machine holds the beat while you own the moment.
timing + agencykeystonebalanced · headless-verified
The most important spike: without a phrase view you can't time a transition, which is the core skill. Free here because we know the arranged structure.

02 · Stem Deckremix-stem-deck.html

Because THIS app composed the record, it splits into real stems for free — Neural Mix, exact and offline.

  • Each record parses (by the .color() tags the engine already writes) into DRUMS / BASS / CHORDS / LEAD / AIR groups, each with mute + solo.
  • One-touch SWAP: A's drums × B's melody (and the reverse) — instant mashups.
  • Makes the clean bass-swap trivial (solo B's bass, mute A's) and closes gaps 3 & 4.
free stemsuniquely possible herebalanced · headless-verified
Turns the app's genome ownership into a feature the big apps charge for. Un-splittable (arranged/remix) pressings fall back to one "full" stem — noted in the UI.

03 · Channel Deckremix-channel-deck.html

The tactile real mixer — continuous knobs instead of kill switches.

  • Per channel: 3-band EQ (HI/MID/LOW) + a filter sweep knob + gain trim (continuous −1…+1).
  • One-touch smooth BASS SWAP ramps A's lows out and B's lows in over ~1s.
  • Beat-synced hold-to-fire FX paddles (stutter / gate / echo-out) — momentary, on the grid.
tactile feelbalanced · headless-verified
Honest about Strudel's limits: there's no true 3-band EQ, so LOW/HI/filter are filter-based approximations. The smooth bass swap is the hero.

04 · Cue & Dropremix-cue-drop.html

The ritual of DJing — cue the next record, then drop it on the phrase.

  • CUE solo-previews a deck (mutes the other) so you can audition the incoming record — an honest stand-in for headphone cueing on a single output.
  • ARM DROP fires on the next 16-bar line (slam or 4-bar fade), with a "drop in N bars" countdown.
  • Performance moves: loop roll (hold), reload (pull-up), spinback (brake).
the ritualbalanced · headless-verified
The clearest answer to the assistance-vs-agency tension: locked beats, human timing. The cue is a single-output approximation — worth an ear to judge if it reads.

Recommendation: land Phrase Deck and Stem Deck first. They close the two biggest gaps (you can't time a mix without a phrase view; you can't do a clean swap or a mashup without stems) and both are only possible because this app owns the genome — a real edge over Serato/rekordbox/djay, not a reskin of them. Channel Deck folds its smooth bass-swap and hold-to-fire paddles into whichever rig ships. Cue & Drop is the highest-risk audition — the single-output cue is an approximation — so try it by ear before committing.

Verified: all four load without error and compose balanced Strudel across full button/slider fuzzing (the same headless bar the app's own test holds). Unauditioned: ears still required — especially the phrase-clock drift (JS clock vs the audio scheduler), the stem-swap musicality, and whether the solo-cue reads as "cueing".

Grounding

Beginner moves & the bass swap / filter fade as the high-reward, low-skill wins: Native Instruments, MusicRadar, ZIPDJ. The sync / assistance-vs-agency debate: Digital DJ Tips, We Are Crossfader, Mixcloud. Stems as a beginner feature (Neural Mix): Algoriddim. Waveform / beatgrid / phrase authenticity: Serato, Algoriddim beatgrids, Mixxx.
