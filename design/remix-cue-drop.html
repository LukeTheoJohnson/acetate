What this spike tests

  • Whether a phrase-locked auto-drop creates genuine tension via the bar countdown — does the countdown prompt the human to act, or does it feel arbitrary?
  • Whether solo-cue (muting the other deck to audition the incoming record) reads clearly enough as a headphone substitute. Real DJ booths have a separate monitor bus; here it is an honest workaround — one audio output, you solo the deck you want to preview.
  • Whether SLAM vs FADE 4 feels like a meaningful artistic choice: SLAM snaps the crossfader instantly on the phrase boundary; FADE 4 ramps gain over the four bars leading up to it via throttled re-evaluates (approximate — re-evaluate latency adds jitter).
  • Whether LOOP ROLL (hold for beat-repeat via .ply("4")) and SPINBACK (ramp setcps to near-zero then hush) read as performance moves or just bugs. Both are momentary and self-cleaning — no lingering timers after release/completion.
  • Whether ↺ RELOAD (re-evaluate the target deck from its stored code) gives a meaningful "pull-up / rewind" feel in context.
  • Known approximations: (a) The phrase clock uses Date.now() elapsed time against masterCps() — it drifts vs Strudel's internal scheduler because evaluate() has its own startup latency. (b) FADE 4 re-evaluates every ~150 ms; actual fade curve is staircase, not smooth. (c) SPINBACK ramps cps via a handful of re-evaluates; real turntable deceleration is smoother. All acceptable for a design spike.
